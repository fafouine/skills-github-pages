# Introduction to urban beekeeping
Urban beekeeping turns rooftops and backyards into busy little neighborhoods. A single hive can host tens of thousands of bees.
Bees visit flowers across the city, helping gardens and trees thrive. In return, they make honey with flavors that shift from block to block. 

## Handling the bees
Beekeeping takes patience and a little routine. A beekeeper checks the hive regularly to make sure the colony has enough food, space, and healthy brood. Inspections are best done on calm, warm days, when many bees are out foraging. A veil, gloves, and gentle movements help keep the work comfortable for both beekeeper and bees.

## Providing adequate food
A healthy hive depends on more than good care inside the box. Bees need nearby flowers that bloom across the seasons, along with clean water and shelter from harsh weather. Planting a mix of native flowers can support them from spring through fall, while avoiding pesticides helps protect the whole colony.

## In it for more than just the honey
Honey is only one part of beekeeping. Bees also pollinate gardens, orchards, and wild plants, making their work valuable well beyond the hive. Harvesting should leave the colony with enough honey for winter, and new beekeepers often benefit from joining a local beekeeping group to learn practical, region-specific advice.
